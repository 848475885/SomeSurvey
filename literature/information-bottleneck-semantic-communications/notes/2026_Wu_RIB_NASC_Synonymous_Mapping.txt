Robust Information Bottleneck Guided
Non-Autoregressive Semantic Communication
With Synonymous Mapping
Mingtong Zhang , Member, IEEE, Haixia Zhang , Senior Member, IEEE,
Dongfeng Yuan , Senior Member, IEEE, and Ping Zhang , Fellow, IEEE

Abstract—Semantic communication emerges as a pivotal
technology for realizing the 6G vision. However, existing
semantic-aware reconstruction systems rely on syntactic-level
loss functions for optimization, failing to focus on the precise
recovery of semantic information and resulting in suboptimal semantic fidelity. Meanwhile, the autoregressive decoding
architecture adopted in text semantic communication introduces
prohibitive high latency. To address these two issues, we propose
a robust information bottleneck (RIB) guided non-autoregressive
semantic communication (NASC) scheme, named RIB-NASC.
First, we pioneer the integration of the RIB criterion into
semantic-aware reconstruction systems, formulating a direct
optimization objective tailored for underlying semantic recovery
and establishing an informativeness-robustness trade-off. Second,
we derive a tractable variational lower bound for the RIB
objective via variational approximation and a novel synonymous
mapping-based semantic posterior estimation strategy. Third,
we design a lightweight non-autoregressive semantic decoder
architecture based on Transformer encoder, enabling high-speed
parallel semantic decoding during inference. Extensive simulation
results demonstrate that the RIB-NASC scheme significantly
outperforms baseline schemes in terms of semantic recovery
performance (BLEU score and sentence similarity) and achieves a
decoding delay reduction of nearly 96.7% compared to traditional
autoregressive decoding.
Index Terms—Semantic communication, information bottleneck, non-autoregressive, synonymous mapping, deep learning.

I. I NTRODUCTION
HE sixth-generation communications (6G) are anticipated to realize deep integration with intelligence and
ubiquitous connectivity [1]. Achieving this vision demands
stringent requirements for delay and transmission rates.
Semantic communication emerges as a critical enabling technology, focusing on extracting essential semantic information
and ensuring its reliable reconstruction at the receiver, thereby
addressing these 6G requirements [2], [3]. By leveraging endto-end joint optimization, semantic communication systems
offer enhanced intelligence, efficiency, and robustness compared to traditional communication paradigms [4].
Recent advances in deep learning (DL) have significantly
advanced semantic information extraction and transmission.
DL-based semantic communication utilizes deep neural networks (DNNs) to learn compact semantic representations from
large datasets and map them to transmission symbols via datadriven methods [5]. Existing research generally falls into two
categories: task-oriented and semantic-aware reconstruction
[6]. Task-oriented systems extract explicit semantic information tailored for specific downstream tasks (e.g., object
detection or classification), directly used for task execution
at the receiver. In contrast, semantic-aware reconstruction
systems capture the underlying global semantics within data
to achieve high semantic-fidelity reconstruction at the receiver.
This paper investigates the second paradigm, specifically
focusing on text sources as the predominant modality in human
communications [7].
Despite the DL-based semantic-aware reconstruction systems having achieved empirical success in diverse wireless
data transmission scenarios [8], [9], [10], [11], [12], some
challenges remain in their practical applications. Firstly, the
invisibility of underlying semantics makes existing works
have to indirectly optimize DL models through syntactic-level
metrics (e.g., cross-entropy or mean squared error (MSE)),
which prioritize token- or pixel-level reconstruction over
semantic fidelity [8], [11]. This discrepancy between optimization objective and the actual demand inevitably results in
suboptimal semantic recovery performance. Secondly, there is
a lack of systematic quantitative analysis for semantic feature
informativeness throughout transmission [13], which hinders
the system from achieving full potential given the available resources. Besides, current text semantic communication

A. Related Works and Motivations
This subsection provides a brief review of the related works
on the semantic-aware reconstruction systems, information
bottleneck (IB)-based semantic communications, and the nonautoregressive decoding.
1) Semantic-Aware Reconstruction Systems: DL-based
semantic-aware reconstruction system typically employs an
encoder-decoder framework, relying on shared training data
as an implicit common knowledge base [16]. The goal of
this framework is to jointly train the encoder and decoder
to improve the semantic recovery performance, and it has
been widely applied to different data modalities. For text data,
Farsad et al. [17] initially established a deep joint sourcechannel coding (JSCC) scheme. Subsequent advancements by
Xie et al. [8] yielded DeepSC, a Transformer-based semantic communication model demonstrating superior semantic
extraction capabilities. Building upon DeepSC, a lightweight
version named L-DeepSC, incorporating parameter quantization and pruning, is proposed for Internet of Things (IoT)
deployment [7]. Besides, there are many works conducted
for diverse scenarios such as multi-user transmission [9],
semantic noise resistance [18], adaptive rate [19], and channel
state information (CSI)-free transmission [10]. For image data,
Bourtsoulatze et al. [20] proposed a convolutional neural
network (CNN)-based deep JSCC scheme, later enhanced
by Kurka et al. [21] through channel feedback mechanisms.
Yang et al. [11] and Cheng et al. [22] respectively developed
new codec backbone networks based on the Swin Transformer
and the Transformer-CNN architecture to enhance the model
capacity. Zhang et al. [23] explored prediction-based adaptive semantic transmission. In the context of speech data,
Weng et al. [24] developed a squeeze-and-excitation network
based on attention mechanism to reconstruct speech signals.
Zhou et al. [12] used Swin Transformer to effectively integrate multi-dimensional semantic features. Han et al. [25]
proposed an end-to-end DL-based transceiver to minimize
semantic redundancy. It is worth noting that the aforementioned works all adopt cross-entropy (for text) or MSE (for
image and speech) as the training loss functions. Essentially,
these loss functions focus more on data recovery metrics (e.g.,
bilingual evaluation understudy (BLEU) score, peak signalto-noise ratio (PSNR)) rather than semantic fidelity. This

fundamental discrepancy between optimization objectives and
semantic preservation motivates the pursuit of semantic-level
optimization.
2) IB-Based Semantic Communications: The IB criterion,
pioneered by Tishby et al. [26], [27], offers an informationtheoretic framework for analyzing the trade-off between
data compression and relevant information preservation in
DNNs. By maximizing the mutual information (MI) with
target variables while minimizing redundancy in compressed
representations, the IB criterion has found applications in
diverse domains, including robustness enhancement against
adversarial attacks [28], representation disentanglement [29],
and domain shift mitigation [30]. Moreover, the advantage
of the IB criterion in the systematic analysis of feature
informativeness has recently prompted its adoption in taskoriented semantic communications. For instance, Shao et al.
[13] employed the IB criterion to model the rate-distortion
trade-off between encoded feature informativeness and image
classification accuracy, enabling efficient task-relevant feature
extraction. Similarly, Wei et al. [31] integrated IB principle
into federated semantic learning to guide feature extraction and
model training. Meanwhile, Lyu et al. [32] and Wang et al. [33]
leveraged IB principle to capture fine-grained robust semantic features and enhance privacy preservation, respectively.
Besides, the recent works [34], [35], [36] adopted a distributed
IB (DIB) variant to optimize multi-user collaborative feature
extraction under rate constraint. Xie et al. [14] developed
an RIB criterion to formulate the trade-off between feature
informativeness and robustness against channel variations.
Although the effectiveness of the IB criterion has been proven
in task-oriented scenarios, it faces limitations when being
applied to semantic-aware reconstruction systems. This is
because IB criterion relies on explicit target variables for MI
computation, which makes it incompatible with underlying
semantic recovery [37]. The latest research [37] has attempted
to modify IB criterion to guide the semantic-aware reconstruction of images in discrete binary channels, but how to apply
IB criterion in more general continuous feature representation
remains unexplored.
3) Non-Autoregressive Decoding: Current text semantic
communication systems predominantly employ autoregressive
models for semantic decoding, relying on left-to-right tokenby-token generation where each prediction is conditioned
on preceding outputs [7], [8], [9], [10]. Such a sequential
nature inevitably leads to inherent delay during inference.
To mitigate this limitation, recent advancements in natural
language processing (NLP) have developed non-autoregressive
decoding, which attempts to generate each token in the target sequence simultaneously and independently to speed-up
the decoding process [38]. Fig. 1 illustrates the comparison
between autoregressive and non-autoregressive decoding. As
shown in Fig. 1, non-autoregressive decoding operates under
the conditional independence assumption that each token
prediction is independent given the encoded source input.
While enabling parallel generation, this strong assumption is
unnatural in reality and leads to low fluency and degraded
generation quality [39]. Recent NLP researchers to alleviate
this issue focus on reducing output diversity in training data

semantic reconstruction.
B. Contributions
This paper proposes a RIB-guided non-autoregressive
semantic communication (NASC) scheme with synonymous
mapping, named RIB-NASC, to improve the semantic recovery performance and reduce the decoding delay of text
semantic transmission. The major contributions of this paper
are summarized as follows:
• We pioneer the integration of the RIB criterion into
semantic-aware reconstruction systems by modeling
underlying semantic sources, and design an RIB optimization objective directly aligned with underlying
semantic recovery. Based on the RIB criterion, we conduct a systematic analysis of the informativeness in
the encoding and decoding processes, and formulate

14861

the informativeness-robustness trade-off in the encoded
representations. As a general and model-agnostic framework, the proposed RIB objective addresses the core
requirements of semantic-aware reconstruction, thereby
facilitating superior recovery performance.
• To address the computational intractability of MI, we
develop a new tractable and differentiable lower bound
of the RIB objective via a variational lower bound, a
variational contrastive log-ratio upper bound (vCLUB)
[45], and a lower bound based on MI neural estimation
[46]. Moreover, to tackle the invisibility of the underlying
semantics, we propose a novel synonymous mappingbased posterior estimation strategy, enabling the joint
approximation of the semantic posterior using the posterior probabilities of multiple synonymous messages.
• We propose a novel Transformer encoder-based nonautoregressive semantic decoder architecture specifically
for text reconstruction. By eliminating sequential dependencies in token prediction, this architecture enables
parallel, fast decoding. Leveraging the profound semantic
understanding capabilities of Transformer encoder, the
proposed decoder serves as an effective platform to
realize the RIB objective. Specifically, it facilitates the
exploration of synonymous expressions at the receiver,
thereby further enhancing semantic recovery performance.
• The effectiveness of the proposed RIB-NASC scheme
is validated under diverse channel conditions, including
the additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN), perfectchannel state information (CSI) Rayleigh fading, and
imperfect-CSI Rayleigh fading channels. Extensive simulation results show that compared to the baseline schemes,
the proposed scheme effectively improves the semantic
recovery performance in terms of the bilingual evaluation
understudy (BLEU) score and sentence similarity, significantly reduces the decoding delay, and exhibits robustness
across different channel conditions.
C. Organization
The rest of the paper is organized as follows. The system model is introduced and the problems are described in

proposed RIB-NASC scheme. In Section IV, we provide
extensive simulation results to evaluate the performance of
the proposed scheme. Finally, Section V concludes the paper.

This paper resorts to the synonymous mapping defined in

Definition 1: Given a message set X = {x1 , x2 , · · · , xQ }

V. C ONCLUSION
In this work, we proposed RIB-NASC, a novel semantic
communication structure that integrates the RIB principle,
synonymous mapping, and non-autoregressive decoding to
address the dual challenges of suboptimal semantic fidelity and
prohibitive decoding latency in semantic-aware reconstruction
systems. By pioneering the RIB framework for semanticaware reconstruction, we established an optimization objective
that directly maximizes semantic information preservation
and formulated an informativeness-robustness trade-off in the
encoded representations. We propose a novel synonymous
mapping-based posterior estimation strategy, together with
which and variational approximation, we further derived a
computationally tractable lower bound for the RIB objective.
With the lower bound, the intractability of MI computation and
semantic invisibility are solved. Furthermore, a lightweight
Transformer encoder-based non-autoregressive decoder architecture was proposed to eliminate sequential dependency in
token generation, achieving parallel decoding while maintaining deep semantic understanding. Extensive simulation
results over diverse channel conditions demonstrate that
RIB-NASC significantly outperforms the state-of-the-art baselines in terms of BLEU score and sentence similarity, while
reducing decoding latency by 96.7% compared to autoregressive approaches. These breakthroughs position RIB-NASC as a
foundational framework for high-fidelity, low-latency semantic
communications in next-generation intelligent networks.
The proposed RIB objective with synonymous mapping
is modality-agnostic and can be generalized to semanticaware reconstruction for other modalities, such as images or
audio. Specifically, in image-based semantic communication,
synonymous samples can be generated via standard data
augmentation techniques (e.g., rotation, brightness adjustment,
or sharpening). However, in contrast to textual sequences
that often exhibit token-level alignment, semantically similar
images may possess significant pixel-level discrepancies. This
disparity poses challenges for directly computing the semantic
posterior from reconstructed images. A viable solution is to
employ projection networks from contrastive learning, which
enable posterior estimation in a mapped semantic space. Such
an extension aligns with our framework and represents a
promising direction for future research.
