Abstract—In this paper, we consider a task-oriented communication paradigm over multi-user broadcast channels for
multi-task edge AI inference, where an edge transmitter performs feature extraction and broadcasts the encoded features
to multiple edge devices, each of which conducts a specific
inference task based on the received signals. However, due to
the heterogeneity of communication links, it still remains an open
problem to balance the trade-off between inference accuracy and
robustness to channel noise over broadcast channels. To address
this issue, we propose a task-oriented broadcast framework
guided by information bottleneck (IB) for feature extraction and
broadcasting, yielding a two-phase network training strategy,
where the former aims to extract task-relevant features for each
task, and the latter is utilized to compress the extracted features
and perform robust broadcasting. Simulation results demonstrate
that the proposed task-oriented broadcast framework can achieve
a better trade-off between inference accuracy and robustness
under various channel conditions than the benchmarks.

I. I NTRODUCTION
Recent progress in edge artificial intelligence (AI) has
boosted a large number of emerging intelligence services
and applications, such as 6G, Internet of Things (IoT), autonomous driving, and Metaverse [1]–[7], which entails realtime, trustworthy, and high-reliable communications. However,
the current Shannon-based communication frameworks only
focus on the correct transmission of as many bits as possible
but neglect the efficient completion of a specific intelligent
task, which is more significant in future cyber communication
systems. The task-agnostic property results in the failure of
the current communication paradigms to support the above
stringent requirements. Recently, task-oriented/semantic-aware
communication is recognized as a promising alternative that
beyonds Shannon’s framework to support emerging intelligent
applications [4]–[6]. Different from the traditional methods,
task-oriented communication concentrates on the extractions
and transmissions of task-relevant information for the completion of specific tasks, which can achieve ultimate compression,
thereby significantly improving communication efficiency.
Motivated by the aforementioned advantages, multiple research works have been devoted to the designs of task-oriented

joint source-channel coding (JSCC) schemes have been developed for wireless image [8] and text [9] transmission.
Besides, a celebrated approach named information bottleneck
(IB) [7], [11], which characterizes the trade-off between informativeness and compression, has been widely adopted in
task-oriented communication. IB aims to extract features that
are informative to conduct inference tasks while keeping the
minimal task-irrelevant information to reduce communication
overhead, which exactly fits into the requirements for future
edge AI. In particular, the authors in [10] proposed an IBbased end-to-end edge AI inference framework to reduce communication cost by introducing sparsification to the extracted
features while maintaining inference performance, and the
authors in [12] extended this work by minimizing energy consumption and latency. Moreover, various types of IB have also
been extensively studied [13], [14]. Particularly, the authors
in [13] proposed robust IB that integrates inference accuracy,
compression, and transmission rate, followed by developing
a discrete JSCC scheme with digital modulation to enhance
the robustness of the edge AI inference systems. Distributed
IB was proposed in [14] for distributed feature encoding to
conduct multi-view edge AI inference tasks.
However, most of the current research works concerning
task-oriented communication focus on the point-to-point [10]
and multi-access [14] communication cases, while few on
the case of multi-user broadcast channel [15], [16]. Specifically, the authors in [15] proposed a JSCC-based wireless
image broadcasting scheme for image reconstruction at the
edge devices, while the authors in [16] developed a featuresdisentangled broadcasting approach, where only user-specific
features are selected and broadcast. However, the broadcast
process in [16] heavily relies on the traditional orthogonal
methods, such as time or frequency division, which cannot
adapt to the future resource-limited communication scenario.
Different from the aforementioned works, for the first time,
we develop a task-oriented broadcast framework guided by
IB for multi-task edge AI inference. Particularly, the edge
transmitter first extracts a task-specific feature for each task,
followed by fusing the extracted features into a compact vector
and broadcasting it to the edge devices in a non-orthogonal
manner without utilizing any digital schemes. Each edge
device interprets the shared message differently and completes
a specific task without requiring to decode the information
concerning the others, which differs from the traditional suc-

B. Problem Formulation
The high-dimensional raw data X is always superfluous to
each task-specific target {Yk }, which requires huge communication and storage overhead for direct transmission. To support
efficient task-oriented communication, the extracted feature Uk
should only keep the task-specific information of task k given
X. The feature extraction process follows IB principle [10],
[11], which approximately supports the extracted feature Uk to
satisfy the minimality and sufficiency condition. The objective
function is formulated as follows:
LIB,k = H(Yk |Uk ) + γI(X; Uk ),

∀k ∈ {1, . . . , K},

(5)

where γ ≥ 0 is an adjustable weight factor.
After extracting the task-relevant features {Uk }, the edge
transmitter encodes them into a compact broadcast signal Z for
downstream transmission, yielding a trade-off between compression and communication rate. Specifically, from the view
of data compression, Z is required to filter out the redundancy
among {Uk }, for which I(Z; U1 , . . . , UK ) , I(Z; U1:K ) is
minimized. The communication rate is ensured by maximizing
the mutual information between the k-th task-relevant feature
Uk and the received signal Ẑk of the k-th edge device,
i.e., I(Uk ; Ẑk ). To balance the trade-off between compression
and communication, we propose a downstream information
bottleneck (DIB) for the multi-task edge AI inference system,
whose goal is to minimize the following objective function:

the entire network at the edge to implement inference instead

V. C ONCLUSION
In this paper, for the first time, we developed a task-oriented
broadcast framework for multi-task edge AI inference over a
degraded Gaussian broadcast channel. To support efficient and
reliable communication, IB principle is introduced for feature
extraction and robust broadcasting. Specifically, assisted by
VA technique, we proposed a two-phase training strategy,
where the former aims to extract task-relevant features for
each task, and the latter is used for feature compression and
robust broadcasting. Simulation results demonstrated that the
proposed task-oriented broadcast scheme can achieve higher
inference accuracy and robustness than the benchmarks under
various SNR conditions.
