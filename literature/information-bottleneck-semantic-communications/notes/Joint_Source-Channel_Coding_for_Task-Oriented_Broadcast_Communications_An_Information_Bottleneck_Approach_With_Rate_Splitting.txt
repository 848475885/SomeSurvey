Abstract—To support efficient and accurate multi-task inference in edge environments, we propose a task-oriented broadcast
communication system that enables an edge transmitter to
serve multiple edge devices with heterogeneous inference tasks.
The proposed system adopts a two-phase design inspired by
Marton’s channel coding with rate splitting and the information
bottleneck principle. In the first phase, a common feature vector
is extracted to capture the shared information across tasks.
In the second phase, task-specific private feature vectors are
generated conditioned on the common feature to preserve unique
task-relevant information. To facilitate interference-robust task
execution, our scheme leverages the intrinsic structural alignment between the task correlations and broadcast channel
properties; specifically, the common and private features are
mapped directly to Marton’s common and private codewords. A
variational approximation method is introduced to optimize the
feature extraction process in both phases, allowing for compact
and informative representations while reducing redundant data

I. I NTRODUCTION
NTEGRATING with AI technologies, the sixth-generation
(6G) networks are enabled to provide more complex intelligent services, especially in the edge environments [2], [3].
An increasing number of IoT devices will be equipped with
AI models, allowing them to communicate more efficiently
and perform on-device AI inference, which leads to the recent
trends in research on edge inference [4]. Edge inference
tasks typically impose stringent Quality of Service (QoS)
requirements, including low latency and high inference accuracy [5], [6], [7]. However, AI model inference may demand
extensive transmission of raw data from edge devices. For
instance, in smart surveillance systems, high-resolution video
streams around 30 Gbps must be transmitted for real-time
analysis [8], [9], and in autonomous driving, multimodal data
streams including radar and camera inputs of 3-40 Gbps must
be communicated for decision-making [10]. Such a massive
volume of data presents significant challenges to the design of
edge inference systems.
As a promising scheme to address these challenges, taskoriented communication has gained significant attention in
recent studies [3], [11], [12], [13]. Unlike conventional
data-oriented communication, which is agnostic to the semantics and only communicates symbols or bits, task-oriented
communication concentrates on delivering the task-relevant
information for task accomplishment (e.g., image/scene classification [14] or visual question answering [15]). On the
one hand, this design scheme eliminates the need to transmit raw data to the receiver by encoding the data into
highly compact feature vectors, thus greatly reducing the
communication overhead. On the other hand, by explicitly
incorporating task-specific metrics and channel conditions into
the encoding procedure, task-oriented design can preserve
inference performance as much as possible, even under poor

To achieve efficient and interpretable task-oriented communications, the information bottleneck (IB) based JSCC
has been widely adopted in task-oriented communication [3],
[21]. The original IB [22], [23], [24] aims to extract features that are informative to conduct inference tasks while
minimizing task-irrelevant information to reduce communication overhead. By adjusting the IB principle to enhance
the transmission reliability (e.g., maximizing an extra term
of mutual information representing the achieved transmission
rate), the transmitter can jointly extract and compress the taskrelevant information while preserving coded redundancy to
resist channel uncertainty. This paves the way for designing
efficient and interpretable task-oriented communication systems. In particular, the authors in [25] proposed an IB-based
end-to-end edge AI inference framework to reduce communication cost by introducing sparsification to the extracted
features while maintaining inference performance, and the
authors in [26] extended this work by minimizing energy
consumption and latency. Moreover, various types of IB have
also been extensively studied [27], [28]. Particularly, the
authors in [27] proposed a robust IB that integrates inference
accuracy, compression, and transmission rate, followed by
developing a discrete JSCC scheme with digital modulation
to enhance the robustness of the edge AI inference systems. Distributed IB was proposed in [28] for distributed
feature encoding to conduct multi-view edge AI inference
tasks.
Apart from the point-to-point scenario, task-oriented design
for the downlink broadcast communication is still in its
infancy. A few studies have investigated the task-oriented
communications for the downlink broadcast scenario [29],
[30], [31], [32], [33], [34]. Specifically, the authors in [29] proposed a JSCC-based wireless image broadcasting scheme for
image reconstruction at the edge devices, while the authors in
[30] developed a features-disentangled broadcasting approach,
where only user-specific features are selected and broadcast.
The work [31] considered a panoramic video transmission
framework where users request diverse fields of view (FoVs).
It exploited the correlation between consecutive video frames
for semantic extraction and employed a rate-splitting transmission strategy to improve communication efficiency. Similarly,
in [32], the authors investigated an energy-efficient semantic
communication framework based on rate-splitting, where computation and communication resources are jointly optimized
to minimize the total energy consumption. Moreover, the
authors in [33] decomposed the broadcast encoding process
into compression and fusion stages to improve communication
efficiency with adaptation to inference tasks and channels. And
the authors in [34] jointly design the uplink gathering and the
downlink broadcasting, where a task-oriented training strategy
is applied to decouple the training into separate stages. Both

JSCC and task-oriented communication design are considered
in [33] and [34] to only transmit task-relevant information.
Besides, instead of training encoders and decoders in an
end-to-end manner, the authors in [33] and [34] separate
the broadcast encoding into stages, which refines the system
design to support diverse downlink inference tasks without
compromising the accuracy. However, their work primarily
focuses on interference-free broadcast scenarios where, in each
time slot, a transmitter exclusively sends information to a
single receiver. More specifically, it assumes that each data
sample has a singular label and will be classified by a specific
intended receiver. In contrast, real-world applications often
involve objects possessing multiple attributes, and various
users may need to extract different pieces of information
from the same object. For instance, in street video analysis,
traffic departments are primarily concerned with vehicle flow,
whereas autonomous vehicles require detailed status information of nearby vehicles. Therefore, investigating broadcast
semantic communications becomes crucial to address these
multifaceted requirements.
In this paper, we address this issue by proposing a novel
task-oriented feature extraction strategy inspired by Marton’s
coding with rate splitting [35], [36]. Marton’s coding was
initially proposed for channel coding, ignoring semantic meaning. However, we identify a strong analogy between its
common and private message components and the characteristics of broadcast semantic communications with multiple
tasks. By integrating the IB principle and Marton’s coding,
we propose a JSCC approach where each input is encoded
into a common feature vector and multiple private feature
vectors. The common feature vector encodes shared information across tasks, while the private feature vectors focus on
task-exclusive content. By conditioning the private encoders
on the common vector, we achieve effective compression and
reduce the dimensionality of task-specific features. This joint
design of common and private encoders enables a better tradeoff between informativeness and communication cost. We
summarize the main contribution as follows.
• With consideration of the task commonality and diversity,
we propose a task-oriented broadcast JSCC framework
that integrates the IB principle and Marton’s coding
with rate splitting. In our framework, the encoders and
decoders are separately designed in two phases to jointly
extract and transmit a common feature vector representing the task commonality and the private feature
vectors representing the task diversity. Specifically, distinct downstream tasks in these systems frequently rely
on shared semantic information while simultaneously
requiring task-specific private semantic content.
• We adopt the variational approximation method to optimize the proposed IB objectives in two training phases.
We first introduce variational distributions to derive a
variational upper bound. Then, we estimate the objectives
with Monte Carlo sampling and train the encoders and
decoders to optimize the IB objectives.
• We evaluate the proposed task-oriented broadcast framework with publicly available real-world datasets. The
results demonstrate that our method achieves the highest

Notably, while rate-splitting has been employed to manage

a significant difference in several key aspects. Firstly, the

attributes from a single, shared source. This fundamental shift
in objectives necessitates a distinct feature extraction strategy:
Both schemes in [31] and [32] identify common features

work exploits inter-task correlations within the same source.

separate stages, we propose a more elegant and integrated
JSCC design. Specifically, we leverage the intrinsic structural alignment between the common-private semantic split
and the codewords in Marton’s coding for broadcast channels. By directly mapping task-specific semantics to channel
codewords, the extracted common and private features are
inherently “channel-aware,” serving simultaneously as channel
input vectors.

II. P RELIMINARIES
A. Task-Oriented Broadcast Communications
We consider a multi-task downlink edge AI inference system consisting of an edge transmitter and K edge devices
as the receivers. In contrast to conventional broadcast communications, the edge inference system typically requires
high inference performance rather than data rates. Directly

14025

transmitting raw data to the receiver may consume more
communication resources, thereby degrading the inference
performance. Moreover, since downlink edge devices may
have different AI inference tasks, delivering raw data in
bits or symbols requires orthogonal resource blocks, which
further compromise the inference performance. Therefore, a
task-oriented communication framework [3], [11] is adopted
to support AI inference tasks. By embedding the goals of
inference tasks into the system design, task-oriented communication only delivers task-relevant information to the
edge devices, thus significantly improving communication
efficiency.
As shown in Fig. 1, the task-oriented framework takes a
learning-based approach, where a pre-trained DNN is split
into two subnets and deployed at the transmitter and receiver,
respectively. At the edge transmitter, the feature extractor
encodes the useful information of raw data x into a feature
vector v. Then, the transmitter broadcasts the feature vector
v to all the downlink edge devices over the wireless channel.
Eventually, at each edge device, the on-device subnet attempts
to infer the label ŷk of the k-th task based on the noisecorrupted feature vector v̂k . Note that rather than retrieving
the original transmitted data x, task-oriented communication
directly obtains the inference result ŷk . Therefore, these two
subnets are usually jointly trained in an end-to-end manner,
which makes the feature vector v into a highly compressed
representation consisting of the task-relevant information.
B. Marton’s Coding in Classical Broadcast Channel

channel with private messages, which can be characterized
by a probability transition function PV̂1 V̂2 |V (v̂1 , v̂2 |v) with a
channel input V and two channel outputs V̂1 , V̂2 . The goal
of communication is that the transmitter conveys two private
messages M1 ∈ {1, . . . , 2nR1 } and M2 ∈ {1, . . . , 2nR2 },
to receivers 1 and 2, respectively, where n is the channel
blocklength and each message Mi is independently and uniformly distributed over the set Mi := {1, . . . , 2nRi }, with Ri
denoting the transmission rate of receiver i, for i ∈ {1, 2}.

Marton’s coding was initially proposed for channel coding
of sending messages without semantic meaning. Interestingly,
we identify a strong analogy between its common and private message components and the characteristics of broadcast
semantic communications with multiple tasks. Specifically,
distinct downstream tasks in these systems frequently rely on
shared semantic information while simultaneously requiring
task-specific private semantic content. Leveraging this inspiration, we propose a Marton’s coding-based JSCC scheme
for broadcast semantic communications to jointly extract and
deliver both common and private semantic information across
noisy channels.
C. Information Bottleneck
The IB principle was first introduced in information theory
[22]. The core idea behind IB is to compress the input variable
X into a new variable V . The variable V should retain as
much information as possible about the inference target Y ,
while discarding the irrelevant information about X. Such V
is obtained by maximizing the mutual information between
V and Y while minimizing the mutual information between
V and X. Mathematically, this is formulated as
min

p(V |X)

− I(V ; Y ) + βI(X; V ),

(2)

where β ≥ 0 is an adjustable weight factor balancing the
trade-off between the feature utility I(V ; Y ) and redundancy
I(X; V ). However, implementing the IB principle in the
edge inference system presents several challenges. First, the
communication scenario involves many downlink devices that
have different inference tasks. Repeatedly solving (2) for each
task is inefficient and not scalable. Further, evaluating the
mutual information terms in (2) is computationally intensive,
especially in high-dimensional spaces, which makes IB even
more difficult to apply in the edge inference system. In the
following sections, we propose a Marton’s coding-based IB to
support the design of the downlink multi-task edge inference

system, and an effective approximation is also proposed to
tackle the intractable mutual information terms.
III. TASK -O RIENTED B ROADCAST BASED ON M ARTON
C ODING AND R ATE S PLITTING
In this section, we propose the system design of the downlink multi-task edge inference system, which incorporates the
IB principle to improve the inference accuracy. We consider
a similar AI inference system as shown in Fig. 1, where an
edge transmitter encodes the raw data into feature vectors and
broadcasts them to K edge devices over the wireless channel.
The task-oriented communication framework is adopted to
deliver encoded features and accomplish the inference tasks
directly, instead of retrieving the raw data sent by the edge
transmitter. Moreover, as mentioned above, the edge inference
system in the downlink broadcast scenario has to serve many
edge devices with multiple inference tasks. Therefore, inspired
by Marton’s coding with rate splitting, we design an encoding
method that separates the encoded features into a common
feature vector and several private feature vectors. The correlation of different downlink tasks is leveraged to encode
the common feature vector, which can be broadcast to all
the devices. Besides the common feature vector, the private
feature vectors are used as supplements and combined with
the common feature vector to accomplish the inference tasks.

Remark 1: While standard IB frameworks typically focus
on point-to-point transmission, our scheme addresses the
complexities of multi-user broadcast scenarios. We decompose semantic information into common (U0 ) and private
(U1 , . . ., UK ) features. This allows the system to exploit
inter-task correlations, significantly reducing redundant computations compared to independent extraction methods. Furthermore, unlike disjoint designs in [30] and [31], where
semantic extraction and transmission are treated as separate
modules, our framework introduces an integrated cross-layer
design. We align the hierarchy of the semantic features
directly with the common and private message streams of
Marton’s coding with rate-splitting. This synergy ensures that
the semantic representation is inherently robust against signal
interference in the broadcast downlink.
Remark 2: In our proposed framework, the common and
private features serve a dual role: they represent semantic
information and function as channel codewords. In a broadcast
channel, Marton’s coding with a common message is the
best-known achievable scheme [36]. It necessitates a common
message that is decoded by all users to facilitate the management of multi-user interference. Moreover, by extracting a
common representation (U0 ) first, we “compress” the correlated information across tasks, allowing the private branches
(Uk ) to focus only on the unique residual details. This reduces
the total dimensionality required compared to a system where
each user must transmit a full, independent feature set.

V. S IMULATION
In this section, we will experimentally verify the effectiveness of the proposed Marton’s coding-based JSCC framework
in the downlink multi-task edge inference system. First, we
will introduce the parameter settings in the experiment, including the hyperparameters used for neural network training
and the dataset used for training. Next, we set three SNR
conditions—low, medium, and high—to present the test accuracy during the neural network training iterations. Finally,
we compare our proposed method with two other methods
under different SNR conditions to demonstrate the robustness
and superior performance of our task-oriented communication
design.
A. Experiment Settings
The dataset used in this experiment is the multi-label
classification dataset MLRSNet [41]. In this dataset, each
data sample (x, y) consists of an input x, which is an RGB
image of size 3 × 256 × 256. These images are high-resolution
satellite images captured from various locations on Earth. The
original dataset contains a total of 109,161 images across 60
classification categories.
Since this dataset is a multi-label classification dataset, to
make the experimental process more controllable and ensure
that the neural network better fits the data, we limit the dataset
size by randomly selecting 18 classification categories. These
18 categories cover various urban infrastructures and natural
landscapes, such as mountains, deserts, oceans, parks, residential areas, and streets. Furthermore, to balance the number of
positive and negative samples in each category, we select only
those samples in which at least three out of the 18 categories
have a classification label of 1 (i.e., a positive example).
Ultimately, we obtained a total of 71,284 samples, which
are split into a training set and a test set in a 0.6:0.4 ratio,
resulting in 42,756 training samples and 28,528 test samples.

We define K = 3 classification tasks, corresponding to three
downlink devices. Specifically, the first six categories out of
the 18 are assigned to the first classification task, the next six
to the second classification task, and the last six to the third
classification task. This experimental design takes into account
the rationality of task distribution in practical applications
while ensuring efficient collaboration between tasks under the
premise of maintaining overall system performance.
The channel model between the edge transmitter and the
edge devices is defined in Equation (4), where the communication quality is characterized by the SNR of each
communication link. This channel model reflects the characteristics of actual wireless transmission and provides a theoretical
basis for subsequent performance analysis. To comprehensively evaluate the performance of our proposed method, we
select the following two schemes as baselines for comparison,
exploring the performance advantages of our proposed system
under different technical approaches:
• DeepJSCC: This method is a deep learning-based
approach that employs deep neural networks to directly
encode input data samples into feature vectors for transmission [18], [25]. In this comparative experiment, we
replace the information bottleneck loss function with a
cross-entropy loss function and train the entire neural
network in an end-to-end manner.
• Digital BPG Encoding: This method utilizes the Better
Portable Graphics (BPG) codec for Source coding and
compression, combined with 5G Low-Density ParityCheck (LDPC) codes for channel coding, setting a
theoretical upper bound on the transmission rate for
digital communication. We considered 5G LDPC codes
with a 1/2 coding rate under quadrature amplitude modulations (QAM) schemes. The goal of the digital BPG
encoding method is to efficiently broadcast compressed
images to edge devices for multi-task inference.
• Rate-Splitting IB: Our proposed method is named RateSplitting IB in the comparative experiment, representing
an information bottleneck-optimized edge inference system based on Marton’s coding and rate-splitting technology. This method aims to enhance inference accuracy
using information bottleneck techniques and improve the
robustness of the inference system under different SNR
conditions.
For each experimental scheme in the comparison, we
primarily evaluate the overall inference performance of the
system based on the final task inference accuracy. Specifically,
an inference result is considered correct only if an edge device
accurately infers all labels in its assigned task. Conversely, if
at least one of the predicted labels is incorrect, the inference
result is deemed incorrect. This evaluation criterion strictly
reflects our high accuracy requirements for inference tasks.
To ensure fairness in the experimental conditions for all
comparison schemes, we adopt a similar deep neural network
architecture across all experiments. It is particularly noteworthy that in the second comparison scheme, the Digital BPG
Encoding method, since this approach directly transmits the
compressed image data, the entire neural network is deployed
at the edge devices to perform inference directly. In contrast,

B. DNN Architecture
In the neural network architecture design, we primarily
consider using the Residual Neural Network (ResNet) [42] as
the fundamental framework. Specifically, we split ResNet18,
which consists of 8 residual blocks and an initial convolutional
layer for processing input, followed by a fully connected
layer for classification output. Each residual block contains
two convolutional layers with a kernel size of 3 × 3, and
the input and output of the residual block are connected
via a residual connection. This residual connection enhances
the stability of deep neural network training. If the input
and output feature sizes of a residual block are inconsistent,
the residual connection includes an additional convolutional
layer with a kernel size of 1 × 1 for downsampling the
input features. Batch normalization (BatchNorm) is applied
within the residual blocks to prevent gradient vanishing and
accelerate model convergence. After batch normalization, the
ReLU activation function is used to obtain nonlinear output
features. Additionally, ResNet18 includes a max pooling layer
with a kernel size of 3 × 3 after the initial convolutional layer,
and an adaptive average pooling layer before the final fully
connected layer to reduce feature dimensionality.
In our designed architecture, the first four residual blocks of
ResNet18, along with the preceding layers, primarily form the
common feature encoder pθ (uc |x), which outputs the common
feature vector uc. The dimensionality of the common feature
vector d is set to 42 × 32 × 32. After extracting the common

14031

feature vector in the first training phase, in the second training
phase, we use the 5th and 6th residual blocks of ResNet18
to construct the private feature encoder pθk (uk |uc , x), which
extracts the private feature vector. The dimensionality of the
private feature vector dk is set to 8 × 16 × 16. During
inference, we use the last two residual blocks of ResNet18
to form the inference network pφk (yk |ûk, ûc, k). In both
the private feature encoder pθk (uk|uc, x) and the inference
network pφk (yk |ûk, ûc, k), we perform feature fusion by concatenating feature vectors. The ResNet-based architecture is
widely utilized in the literature for its robust feature extraction
and its optimal balance between depth and computational
efficiency. This choice specifically aligns with the stringent
energy and hardware constraints inherent in edge deployment
scenarios, such as satellite-based remote sensing, where the
implementation of ultra-deep models is often precluded by
limited onboard processing resources.
To ensure fair comparisons, we utilize the same backbone
network and maintain equal the total dimensionality for the
transmitted features across all evaluated schemes. Specifically,
in the baseline methods where no distinction is made between
common and private features, the dimensionality of the vector

VI. C ONCLUSION
In conclusion, this paper proposed a task-oriented broadcast
communication system based on a rate-splitting IB framework to support multi-task edge AI inference. By separating
the encoded features into a common feature vector and
multiple task-specific private feature vectors, the proposed
two-phase design effectively captures the commonality and
diversity among tasks. Variational approximation methods
were employed to optimize the information bottleneck objectives for both phases, enabling compact and informative
feature extraction while reducing communication overhead.
Experimental evaluations on real-world datasets demonstrated
that the proposed method achieves superior inference accuracy
and robustness under diverse channel conditions, outperforming traditional digital compression and JSCC coding schemes.
This validates the effectiveness and practicality of the proposed
approach in multi-device edge AI systems. Future research
directions include analyzing the theoretical gap of the variational IB objective in specific analytical regimes, as well
as extending the proposed two-phase training framework to
fading and more dynamic broadcast environments.
