Information Bottleneck Guided
Joint Source-Channel Coding with HARQ

2025 IEEE Wireless Communications and Networking Conference (WCNC) | 979-8-3503-6836-9/25/$31.00 ©2025 IEEE | DOI: 10.1109/WCNC61545.2025.10978742

Haoxuan Zhang, Lunan Sun, Caili Guo, Senior Member, IEEE and Yang Yang, Member, IEEE

Abstract—Deep joint source-channel coding (JSCC) with hybrid automatic repeat request (HARQ) for image transmission
has attracted increasing attention due to its flexibility and
high efficiency. Existing researches mainly focus on minimizing
the distortion of mutiple retransmissions while ignoring the
redundancy in retransmitted signal and such redundancy may
lead bandwidth waste and reconstruction quality degradation.
In this paper, we propose an information bottleneck (IB) guided
deep JSCC with HARQ system (HARQ-IBJSC), which aims
at improving the reconstruction quality by compressing the
redundancy in retransmitted signal. In particular, we first design
a new IB objective for deep JSCC with HARQ system, which
simultaneously reduces redundancy in the retransmitted signal
and minimizes image transmission distortion. Since the mutual
information terms in the designed IB objective is intractable, we
then derive a differentiable lower bound on the IB objective and
use the bound as the loss function of HARQ-IBJSC. Experimental
results show that the proposed HARQ-IBJSC system can increase
PSNR by up to 1 dB.
Index Terms—Information bottleneck, joint source-channel
coding, HARQ

I. I NTRODUCTION
With the rapid development of modern communications,
deep learning (DL) has been utilized in communication systems to improve the flexibility and efficiency of traditional
communication architectures [1], [2]. In particular, integrating
DL enabled joint source-channel coding (JSCC) [3], [4] has
attracted increasing attention as a promising alternative to the
conventional shannon separate source-channel coding (SSCC)
method [5]. Benefiting from the fitting capability of neural
networks, deep JSCC can automatically learn key information
from the data and outperform traditional SSCC in terms of
perceived visual quality [3], [6]. However, when the channel
condition is harsh, deep JSCC may still fail due to the severely
fading and noise interference [7], [8].
To improve the reliability of deep JSCC, a few recent studies
introduced hybrid automatic repeat request (HARQ) into deep
JSCC to enhance the reconstruction quality by exploiting

authors in [7] first proposed an deep JSCC with HARQ
system (HARQ-JSCC) using autoencoder. They considered
the sentence transmission and minimized the transmission
mean squared error (MSE) of multiple retransmissions. The
proposed HARQ-JSCC outperforms deep JSCC and SSCC
based HARQ that combines Huffman coding with low density
parity check codes (LDPC). The authors in [8] proposed a new
HARQ-JSCC system with single encoder and improved the
reconstruction quality of HARQ-JSCC through the integration
of a self-attention module.
Overall, existing works on HARQ-JSCC aim at minimizing
the distortion of multiple retransmissions. However, existing
methods ignore the redundancy in the retransmitted signal,
which may lead to the transmission of overlapping information in the retransmission link. To compress the redundant
information, the authors in [9] introduced an informationtheoretic principle for neural networks, termed as information
bottleneck (IB). With the guidance of IB, neural networks are
able to extract information relevant to the target task while
compress information irrelevant to the target task.
The main contribution of this work is an IB guided HARQJSCC system (HARQ-IBJSC). The proposed HARQ-JSCC introduced a new form of IB, which employs the received information to eliminate retransmission redundancy, thus improving
reconstruction quality. To the best of authors’ knowledge, this
is the first work that applies IB into HARQ-JSCC system.
In particular, we establish a new IB objective for HARQJSCC system, which simultaneously compresses redundancy
in the retransmitted signal and minimizes image transmission
distortion. Since the mutual information terms in the designed
IB objective is intractable, we derive a differentiable lower
bound on the IB objective and use the bound as the loss
function of HARQ-IBJSC. Experimental results show that the
propoesd HARQ-IBJSC can increase PSNR by up to 1 dB.
The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In Section
II, the system model and the loss function of existing works
are described. The proposed HARQ-IBJSC is presented in
Section III. Experimental results are shown in Section IV. Final
conclusions are drawn in Section V.

A. System Model
As shown in Fig. 1, we consider a point-to-point image
transmission system, which consists of two transmission links,
denoted as transmission link and retransmission link. An input
image with size H (Height) × W (Width) × C (Channel) is
represented as a vector x ∈ Rn , where R represents the set
of real numbers and n = H × W × C . The joint source
channel encoder (referred to as the encoder) in transmission
link encodes x and the encoding function Eφ1 : Rn → Ck
leads to a vector of complex-valued channel input symbol z1 ∈
Ck , where k is the size of channel input symbol. The encoding
process can be expressed as:
z1 = Eφ1 (x) ,

(1)

where φ1 is the parameter set of the encoder neural network
and C denotes the set of complex numbers. To satisfy the
average power constraint at the encoder, k1 E (z1 z1∗ ) ≤ 1 is
imposed, where z1∗ denotes the conjugate transpose of z1 .
z1 is transmitted over a noisy channel represented by the
function η : Ck → Ck . We consider two widely used channel
models: (i) the additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN) channel, and (ii) the Rayleigh fading channel. For the AWGN
channel, the channel output symbol ẑ1 ∈ Ck received by the
joint source-channel decoder (referred to as the decoder) can
be expressed as:
ẑ1 = ηn (z1 ) = z1 + ω,

(2)

where the vector ω ∈ Ck consists of independent identically

ẑ1 = ηh (z1 ) = hz1 + ω,

The decoder in the transmission link uses a decoding

x̂1 = Dθ1 (ẑ1 ) = Dθ1 (η (Eφ1 (x ))) ,

When the decoder in the transmission link fails to decode

negative acknowledged (NAK) signal to the transmitter. After
receiving a NAK signal, the encoder in retransmission link
encodes x again and the encoding function Eφ2 : Rn → Ck
leads to a vector of complex-valued channel input symbol
z2 ∈ Ck . z2 is transmitted over a channel with the same
channel conditions as the transmission link channel. The
channel output symbol ẑ2 is expressed as:
ẑ2 = η (z2 ) = η (Eφ2 (x )) .

(5)

The decoder in retransmission link concatenates ẑ2 to ẑ1 ,

x̂2 = Dθ2 (ẑ1 , ẑ2 ) = Dθ2 (η (Eφ1 (x )) , η (Eφ2 (x ))) ,

where x̂2 ∈ Rn is an reconstruction of the original image x
and θ2 is the parameter set of the decoder neural network in
retransmission link.
B. Loss Function
Akin to [6], the image size n, the channel input size k
and R = k/n are treated as the source bandwidth, the
channel bandwidth and bandwidth ratio, respectively. Under
a certain R, the existing HARQ-JSCC works primarily aim at
minimizing the transmission distortion as follows:
L = Ep(x,x̂1 ,x̂2 ) [d (x, x̂1 ) + d (x, x̂2 )],

(7)

where p (x, x̂1 , x̂2 ) represents the joint probability distribution
of x and x̂1 , x̂2 , and d (·, ·) is the distortion between x and
x̂1 , x̂2 .
However, existing methods fail to consider the correlation
of ẑ1 and z2 , which may transmit the overlapping semantic
information in the retransmission link, thus introducing information redundancy.
Therefore, to make this framework more efficient in reconstructing images, two issues need to be addressed. (i) How
to measure the correlation of ẑ1 and z2 ? (ii) How to use ẑ1
to guide the encoding process of the retransmission link to
eliminate redundancy while improving reconstruction quality?
To solve the above problems, we propose a new form of IB
objective for HARQ-JSCC system.

D. Training process
The entire training process of the IB guided HARQ-JSCC
system can be divided into three steps.
Step 1: Eφ1 and Dθ1 are jointly trained using d (x, x̂1 )
as loss function to improve the reconstruction quality of the
transmission link.
Step 2: Eφ1 , Dθ1 and Eφ2 are fixed. Dθ2 and Eγ are alternately trained using loss function (12) to calculate accurate
mutual information estimation.
Step 3: Dθ2 and Eγ are fixed. Eφ2 is trained using (18)
as loss function to improve the reconstruction quality of the
retransmission link.

two widely used image quality metrics, PSNR and SSIM to
measure the reconstruction quality.

robustness of the HARQ-IBJSC. In particular, we vary beta
between 0 and 0.1 to impose different constraint on the amount
of redundancy between z2 and ẑ1 . All systems are trained
under the same SNRtrain = −5 dB. According to Fig. 3(d), the
performance of HARQ-IBJSC is close to HARQ-JSCC when
β = 0. This is because that the loss of HARQ-IBJSC descends
to the summation of MSE which is similar with that of HARQJSCC. However, when β is larger than 0, HARQ-IBJSC
shows better reconstruction performance compared to HARQJSCC. This demonstrates the effectiveness of our designed IB
objective. In addition, when β = 1 × 10−3 , the performance
of HARQ-IBJSC is better than that when β = 1 × 10−4 . This
demonstrates that the proper compression of the redundancy
between z2 and ẑ1 is helpful for improving the reconstruction
quality and robustness to channel variations.
Fig. 4 presents visualization examples of reconstructed
images, showcasing the performance of our proposed HARQIBJSC. The DL-based system are trained under SNRtrain = 5
dB and test under the same SNR, which is corresponding
to the BPP R = 0.1. As shown in Fig. 4, we can observe
the detail of the reconstructed images clearly, such as license
plate, pedestrian and the edge of the road. This is because with
the guidance of the designed IB objective, HARQ-IBJSC can
preserve crucial semantic information, thus achieving richer
and more photo-realist textures than the other systems.
V. C ONCLUSION
In this work, we have proposed an IB guided HARQJSCC system, which aims at improving the reconstruction
quality by compressing redundancy in retransmitted signal.

Specifically, we have proposed a new IB objective for HARQJSCC system, which simultaneously reduces redundancy in
the retransmitted signal and minimizes image transmission
distortion. To optimize the proposed objective, we have derived
a differentiable lower bound on the IB objective and use the
bound as the loss function of HARQ-IBJSC. Experimental
results showed that the proposed HARQ-IBJSC system can
significantly improve the robustness to the channel variation
and increase the reconstruction quality.
